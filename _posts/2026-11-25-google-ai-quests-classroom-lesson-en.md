Google officially targets kids aged 11 to 14 with its educational AI quests, yet even my fourth graders finished both Ukrainian scenarios. Once you run AI Quests in class across grades 4 to 7, it quickly feels more like a structured project than a light game.

I covered device performance separately in my [Google AI Quests tablet review](https://sivochka.com/blog/google-ai-quests-tablets-fps/). Here I focus on classroom timing, where students slow down, and which of the two quests breaks at the finish line with a {% include term.html id="softlock" text="softlock" %}.

## How AI Quests in class play out across grades 4 to 7

One quest takes 15 to 25 minutes, so it fits neatly into a standard 45-minute lesson with room for a short wrap-up. No prior AI knowledge or coding background is needed, as noted in the [official announcement by the Ministry of Education and Science of Ukraine](https://mon.gov.ua/news/google-zapuskaie-ukrainskoiu-bezkoshtovnu-hru-ai-quests-pro-shtuchnyi-intelekt-dlia-shkoliariv).

The gap between grade levels shows up fast. Students in grades 6 and 7 worked noticeably faster than grades 4 and 5. The biggest surprise for kids was reading: to understand each step and tune the model, they actually had to read the on-screen text carefully, which shocked many of them. Content-wise, the scenarios fit anyone from grade 4 upward, and I would gladly assign them to high schoolers too.

## Two live quests and what students actually do

Guided by the virtual researcher Professor Skye, students collect and inspect data, train an AI model, review mistakes, and adjust their choices ([Ministry of Digital Transformation](https://thedigital.gov.ua/news/education/hrayte-ta-vyvchayte-shtuchnyy-intelekt-google-zapuskaye-ai-quests-dlia-ukrayinskykh-pidlitkiv)). Two quests are available in Ukrainian right now, with a third one about brain research and neural connections announced for later.

| Quest | Topic and training data | Classroom stability |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Market Cove (Topky Rynok) | Flood forecasting from rainfall and river levels | Softlock in task 5 with trading posts |
| Dusk Dunes (Prysmerkovi Diuny) | Early eye disease detection (diabetic retinopathy) | Finished with no softlocks |

I only skimmed Google's teacher guide and skipped generating student certificates, focusing our lesson time on completing both scenarios hands-on.

## The task 5 softlock explained for parents

For parents who do not play video games, a quick definition helps. A softlock is a dead end inside a running game: the screen has not frozen and buttons still respond, yet a {% include term.html id="bug" text="bug" %} makes it impossible to finish the level.

That is exactly what happened in the fifth and final task of the flood quest, where trading posts stopped moving. Reloading the page got students unstuck, but half of them hit the exact same lock again whenever they placed a post in the wrong spot. In the worst case, a student had to reload after every single attempt.

{% include gallery.html id="quests" caption="The task 5 bug in the flood quest and a finished quest on a school tablet" %}

**Because of the task 5 bug, it is better to skip the flood quest for now.** If you still want to introduce how AI models learn from data on this platform, start with Dusk Dunes: my classes had zero softlocks there and reached the ending cleanly.
