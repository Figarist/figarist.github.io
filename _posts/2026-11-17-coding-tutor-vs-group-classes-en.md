Parents who decide that their child should learn programming quickly face the next question: **a private coding tutor or a group course at an IT school?**

Let me be upfront: I teach 1-on-1, so I am not a neutral observer. But I am also a practicing school computer science teacher, which means I work with whole classes as well as with individual students. I see what groups do well and where they struggle. In this article I try to be fair to both formats, including the situations where I would honestly recommend a group.

If you have not yet decided *what* your child should learn (Scratch, Python, Unity or school computer science), read the [parent guide to choosing a programming track](https://sivochka.com/blog/how-to-choose-programming-for-child/) first. This article is about the *format*.

---

## What is the real difference between a tutor and a group course?

There are strong and weak teachers in both formats. The real difference is **how the teacher's time and attention are shared**.

In a group, the teacher divides the lesson between several children. The programme is planned for everyone, and the pace has to suit the group as a whole. In 1-on-1 lessons, the whole hour belongs to one child: the pace changes from lesson to lesson, and the content can be built around that child's own project. The price of that is that there are no classmates.

---

## Tutor vs group class: how do they compare?

| Factor | Private tutor (1-on-1) | Group course / IT school |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Teacher's attention** | The whole lesson is about one child | Shared between all children in the group |
| **Pace** | Adjusted every lesson | Set by the programme and the group |
| **Content** | Can be built around the child's own project | A shared curriculum, often the same projects for everyone |
| **Social side** | Just the child and the teacher | Peers, teamwork, friendly competition |
| **Cost per hour** | Usually higher | Usually lower per hour |
| **Schedule** | A slot agreed individually | A fixed group timetable |
| **Quality depends on** | One specific person | The school's system and the particular teacher assigned |
| **Parent communication** | Directly with the teacher | Often through a manager or a group chat |

Neither column wins on every line. That is why the right answer depends on your child, not on the format.

## When is a group course the better choice?

Here are the situations where I genuinely think a group can work better than a tutor:

- **Your child is very social and motivated by other kids.** Some children work harder when they can show their game to friends or compare results. Being part of a group can be the main reason they come back each week.
- **You just want to see whether coding interests your child at all.** If you are not sure yet, a group can be a lower-cost way to find out.
- **Your child needs to practise working in a team.** Sharing ideas, splitting tasks and explaining your work to peers are real skills that 1-on-1 lessons do not train in the same way.
- **The activity needs shared equipment.** An offline robotics club, where children build with physical kits together, is hard to replace with an online lesson.
- **Your child is confident and independent.** If they keep up easily, ask questions without hesitation and rarely get stuck, they may not need individual attention all the time.
- **Budget is the deciding factor right now.** Regular group lessons may be more realistic than individual ones, and regular practice matters more than the perfect format.

## When does a private tutor make more sense?

From my experience, 1-on-1 lessons help most in these cases:

- **Your child is shy** and will not ask a question in front of others, even when they are completely lost.
- **Your child is ahead of or behind the group.** A fast learner gets bored waiting; a slower one quietly falls behind. In 1-on-1 lessons we simply move at the child's real speed.
- **There is a specific goal**, such as building their own game. For example, in [Unity & C# mentoring](https://sivochka.com/tutoring/unity/) a teen can work on one project for a long time, which is hard to do in a shared curriculum. I describe how that works in [Unity and C# 1-on-1 mentoring for kids and teens](https://sivochka.com/blog/unity-csharp-mentoring-for-kids/).
- **There are gaps in school computer science** that need targeted work, which is what my [computer science tutoring](https://sivochka.com/tutoring/informatics/) is for.
- **Your family needs a flexible schedule** or lives in a different time zone from the school.
- **Your child gets distracted in a busy group** but focuses well when someone works with them directly.

## Decision checklist: tutor or group?

Answer these questions honestly about your child. Each answer points to one format.

- [ ] Does my child ask questions when they do not understand, even in front of others? *(Yes → group can work. No → tutor.)*
- [ ] Do they enjoy working next to other children? *(Yes → group. No → tutor.)*
- [ ] Do they have something specific they want to build or a school topic to fix? *(Yes → tutor.)*
- [ ] Are they clearly ahead of or behind other kids their age in coding? *(Yes → tutor.)*
- [ ] Do we only want to test their interest for now? *(Yes → group.)*
- [ ] Is cost the main limit at the moment? *(Yes → group.)*
- [ ] When they get stuck, do they keep going on their own? *(Yes → group can work. No → tutor.)*
- [ ] Does our schedule or time zone make fixed group times difficult? *(Yes → tutor.)*

If your answers are split, that is normal. Many families start with one format and change later.

## What should parents ask before signing up for either format?

Whichever you choose, these questions save a lot of disappointment:

1. **Who exactly will teach my child?** Can I see their background or their own projects?
2. **How many children are in the group?** (For a group course.)
3. **Can I see a project a student has actually built?**
4. **What happens if we miss a lesson?**
5. **What do I pay upfront, and what happens to the money if we stop?**
6. **Is the first lesson free or paid, and what happens in it?**

## Can you combine a group course and a tutor?

Yes. A child can enjoy a group club for the social side and take individual lessons for a specific project or a difficult school topic. Or they can start in a group and move to 1-on-1 lessons once they know what they want to build.

## FAQ: coding tutor vs coding class

**Is a private tutor always better than a group?**
No. A tutor gives more individual attention, but a group gives peers, teamwork and usually a lower price per hour. The better format is the one that fits your child.

**Is a 60-minute 1-on-1 lesson too intense for a young child?**
For the youngest students, for example in [Scratch lessons](https://sivochka.com/tutoring/scratch/) from age 6, I keep the pace calm and build the lesson around the child's favourite games or cartoons, so the hour feels like making something, not like a test.

**Can my child move from a group course to 1-on-1 lessons midway?**
Yes. We start from what they already know. In Unity, a student can even bring an existing project, and we continue from there.

**How do I compare prices fairly?**
I can only speak for my own rate. When comparing, look at how much individual attention and practice your child gets for each hour you pay for, not only at the price.
